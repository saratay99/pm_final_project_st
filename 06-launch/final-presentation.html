  • Bad signal to watch for e.g. high reach, zero signups = message-market mismatch: High Spotlight visibility, but few qualified plays and little repeat use. Wanderers see the rail but still don’t choose, suggesting the picks or presentation aren’t reducing choice overload.

Faster starts, rapid abandonment: Time-to-Play improves, but viewers quickly quit. The rail speeds up decisions without delivering satisfying choices.

Strong first-week use, weak returns: Adoption spikes, then fades. The feature may be a novelty rather than a habit.

More playback failures: Interest exists, but technical friction prevents viewing. Investigate reliability before changing the message or picks.

Module 6

Individual Insights

Where the work was hardest, the moment my thinking changed, and what I learned.

Friction points

Initially the product specific technical terminology and the technical specifications utilized during some of the labs. After reviewing the course materials and working through the labs, it became easier to understand the context and methods of application.

Aha moment

As the course honed in on the identified persona and seeking to build out a relevant and effective product for their requirement and experience. This helped shaped my understanding on how to approach all the follow-up work as it relates to decision making and tailoring on what is essential to build and for what purpose.

Key learnings

How it's essential to define the goals early on as it ties back to your persona or intended audience. Oftentimes, you're asked to build out products for the sake of creation or as a stopgap solution but ultimately it doesn't serve a sustainable purpose and can get decommissioned.
